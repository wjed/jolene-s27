# Cold-emailing a principal investigator

The highest-return thing on this site, and almost nobody does it.

Many summer programs ask applicants to name preferred mentors, and a fair number
of placements come down to a faculty member saying "I would take this student."
At NIH it is the whole process. The Summer Internship Program has no central
selection: individual investigators choose their own interns, starting in early
January, so an email to an NIH lab is part of applying.

Several places on this site are reachable mainly this way: UCSF pediatrics,
which has no undergraduate application; the UC Davis MIND Institute, where a
direct lab email works better than the central application; and the places near
home that have not posted a 2027 summer application with a deadline (Children's
National, Georgetown, George Washington and Inova). One is the opposite: the
Boston Children's epilepsy internship asks that questions go only to its
internship address and never to lab members, so apply through its page instead.
That page still shows last cycle's dates, so check it from early January.

A good cold email takes fifteen minutes. Send three or four per program you
seriously want, earliest deadline first, and three to five to NIH labs, ideally
before Thanksgiving and at the latest before winter break. Start with the
programs rated Strong fit or Good fit, and read the For you line on each before
you write.

---

## The rules

1. **Short.** Under 200 words. They are reading it on a phone between meetings.
2. **Specific.** Name one of their papers and say something true about it.
   Generic praise is worse than none.
3. **Ask for one thing.** Not "please mentor me for the summer", just whether
   they are taking a summer student.
4. **Answer "who pays" and "when" before they ask.** Those are the first two
   questions in a PI's head. Name the program you are applying through and say
   that it pays your stipend, then give the dates you are free. An email that
   answers both reads as a student who can be slotted into a plan. One that
   leaves them open reads as a request for money and a scheduling problem.
5. **Attach the CV.** One PDF, named `Lastname-Firstname-CV.pdf`, built from the
   [CV skeleton](../templates/cv-skeleton.md).
6. **Subject line that survives a crowded inbox.**
7. **No follow-up for ten days.** Then exactly one, and never a third. If day
   ten falls over the holidays, send it in the first week of January.

## Template

> **Subject:** Summer 2027 [PROGRAM] applicant interested in [THEIR TOPIC]
>
> Dear Dr. [LAST NAME],
>
> I am a Penn State junior majoring in Integrative Science with a psychology
> minor, and I am applying to [PROGRAM NAME] for summer 2027. I would like to
> work in your lab.
>
> I read your [YEAR] paper on [THE FINDING, not the title]. [ONE SENTENCE THAT
> SHOWS YOU READ IT.]
>
> Since August 2025 I have been a research assistant in Dr. [YOUR PI]'s
> depression and anxiety lab, running participant sessions, coding data,
> troubleshooting study protocols and working on technology-based emotion
> tracking. I hold CITI certificates in social and behavioral human subjects
> research and Good Clinical Practice. I am also a volunteer EMT and have
> responded to more than 100 emergency calls.
>
> [PROGRAM NAME] pays a stipend, so I would not need lab funding. I am free full
> time from [START DATE] to [END DATE]. [IF YOU CAN COMMUTE:] I live in Northern
> Virginia, so I do not need housing.
>
> Are you taking a summer student in 2027? If the application asks for a
> preferred mentor, I would like to name you, with your permission.
>
> My CV is attached. Thank you for your time.
>
> [FULL NAME]
>
> Penn State, Class of 2028
>
> [EMAIL] · [PHONE]

**The paper sentence** is the one only you can write: what surprised you, a
question it raised, or how it connects to the mood and emotion work in your own
lab.

**The background paragraph** fits the labs you will email most, the ones that
study people. Keep the CITI sentence for all of them: that training is the gate
on work with participants or patients, and most undergraduates have none. Then
fit the last sentence to the lab:

- **Clinical or hospital research** (Children's National, Inova, an NIH clinical
  study): keep the EMT sentence and add "In summer 2026 I was a medical
  assistant in family medicine, seeing up to 26 patients a day and handling
  vital signs, blood draws and vaccines." If you have added the biomedical CITI
  course by then, name it in the CITI sentence, since hospital studies mostly
  run on that track. Trim elsewhere to stay under 200 words.
- **A pediatric lab**: swap in "I have also shadowed a pediatrician for more
  than five years."
- **A lab with no patient contact**: swap in "As a teaching assistant for a
  300-student introductory psychology course, I started review sessions on my
  own."
- **A bench lab**: replace the CITI and EMT sentences with "I have not done
  bench research yet, but I have taken organic chemistry and biochemistry, and I
  have been a Biology Learning Assistant for 80 students."

**The stipend sentence** is the one most applicants leave out, and it changes
how the whole email reads. Check the program page first: most programs on this
site pay a stipend, but some post no amount and a few pay nothing, so write only
what is true. Where you find no program to name, as so far at Georgetown,
George Washington and Inova, cut that sentence and the preferred-mentor line.
Make the subject "Summer 2027 research, undergraduate interested in [THEIR
TOPIC]", say in your first line that you are looking for summer research, and
ask whether the lab has a summer program you could apply through.

**The housing sentence** is for labs you can reach from home: NIH, Janelia (if
Penn State is a partner school for HHMI's Cech Fellows Program, its route for
summer undergraduates), Inova and anywhere in DC. Baltimore is about 70 minutes
each way, so keep it there only if you mean to make that drive, and cut it
everywhere else. The
[living-at-home rule](../guides/reading-a-program-page.md) is why the labs near
home are worth extra emails.

**For NIH**, name the Summer Internship Program and leave out the stipend
sentence: NIH sets the stipend, and its PIs know how it is paid. Say instead
whether you have submitted the application, which closes January 26 at noon
Eastern. Dates are set with your PI, anywhere between May 10 and August 31.
Name the same PIs in your cover letter.

## Finding the right PI

Before you search, ask the people you have worked with whether they can point
you to anyone doing the work you want: your lab's PI, the pediatrician you have
shadowed for more than five years, the doctors from your medical assistant
summer. An introduction from any of them beats a cold email.

1. Open the program page, find the faculty or mentor list.
2. No list? Search the department site for your topics: child anxiety and
   depression, developmental psychology, developmental and behavioral
   pediatrics, pediatric neurology. For NIH, search the
   [Intramural Research Program](https://irp.nih.gov) by topic and look at the
   institute: NICHD for child health, NIMH for mood and anxiety, NINDS for
   neurology.
3. Open two or three recent papers from each candidate on PubMed. If the
   abstracts bore you, you have saved yourself a summer.
4. Check they are actually active. A lab whose last paper was 2019 may be
   winding down.
5. Get the email from the faculty page, not from a paper's corresponding-author
   line, which is often stale.

## What a reply looks like

- **"Yes, name me"**: excellent. Put it in the application immediately and say
  so explicitly: *"Dr. X has agreed to host me."* That line is worth a lot. At
  NIH, a yes means the PI will look for your application, so make sure it is
  submitted.
- **"I am not taking students but talk to Dr. Y"**: a warm introduction. Use it.
- **"Apply through the central program"**: neutral, but you are now a name they
  have seen rather than one of 400 files.
- **Silence**: the normal outcome. Not personal. Send more.

## Worth knowing

Expect most emails to go unanswered. Ten emails producing one "yes" is a good
rate, and that one yes can decide the summer. The failure mode is not sending
them at all.
