# Cold-emailing a principal investigator

The highest-return thing in this whole repo, and almost nobody does it.

Many summer programs ask applicants to name preferred mentors, and a fair number
of placements come down to a faculty member saying "I would take this student."
At NIH it is the whole process. The Summer Internship Program has no central
selection: individual investigators choose their own interns, starting in early
January, so an email to an NIH lab is part of applying. Several entries on this
list, the Boston Children's epilepsy lab, the UC Davis MIND Institute, the NIA
Alzheimer's centers, UCSF pediatrics, and the hospitals near home that post no
summer application, such as Georgetown and Inova, are effectively *only*
reachable this way.

A good cold email takes fifteen minutes. Send three or four per program you
seriously want, and three to five to NIH labs before winter break.

---

## The rules

1. **Short.** Under 200 words. They are reading it on a phone between meetings.
2. **Specific.** Name one of their papers and say something true about it.
   Generic praise is worse than none.
3. **Ask for one thing.** Not "please mentor me for the summer", just whether
   they are taking a summer student.
4. **Answer "who pays" and "when" before they ask.** Those are the first two
   questions in a PI's head. Name the program you are applying through and say
   that it pays your stipend, then give the dates you are free. An email that
   answers both reads as a student who can be slotted into a plan. One that
   leaves them open reads as a request for money and a scheduling problem.
5. **Attach the CV.** One PDF, named `Lastname-Firstname-CV.pdf`.
6. **Subject line that survives a crowded inbox.**
7. **No follow-up for ten days.** Then exactly one, and never a third.

## Template

> **Subject:** Summer 2027 [PROGRAM] applicant interested in [THEIR TOPIC]
>
> Dear Dr. [LAST NAME],
>
> I am a [YEAR] at [SCHOOL] studying [MAJOR], and I am applying to [PROGRAM NAME]
> for summer 2027. I am writing because I would like to work in your lab.
>
> I read your [YEAR] paper on [SPECIFIC TOPIC, the actual finding, not the
> title]. [ONE SENTENCE THAT SHOWS YOU READ IT: something that surprised you, a
> question it raised, how it connects to what you want to understand.]
>
> My background: [ONE SENTENCE, relevant coursework, lab experience, technique].
> [IF APPLICABLE: a sentence on what you can already do that is useful:
> participant sessions, data coding, R or Python, data entry on a clinical
> study. Name your CITI human subjects and Good Clinical Practice training here.
> It is the gate on work with patients or study participants, and most
> undergraduates have not done it.]
>
> [PROGRAM NAME] pays its interns a stipend, so I would not need funding from
> the lab. I am free from [START DATE] to [END DATE], full time. [IF YOU COULD
> COMMUTE: I live in Leesburg, so I do not need housing.]
>
> Are you taking a summer student in 2027? If the program application should name
> a preferred mentor, I would like to name you, but only if that would be
> welcome.
>
> My CV is attached. Thank you for your time.
>
> [FULL NAME]
> [SCHOOL], Class of [YEAR]
> [EMAIL] · [PHONE]

The stipend sentence is the one most applicants leave out, and it changes how
the whole email reads. Check the program page first: nearly every program on
this list pays a stipend, but write only what is true. If there is no program
behind the email, as with a hospital that posts no summer application, leave
that sentence out and ask instead whether the lab has a summer program you
could apply through.

For NIH, name the NIH Summer Internship Program, say whether you have already
submitted the application, and name the same PIs in your cover letter.

## Finding the right PI

1. Open the program page, find the faculty or mentor list.
2. No list? Search the department site for the research area. For NIH, search
   the [Intramural Research Program](https://irp.nih.gov) by topic and look at
   the institute: NICHD for child health, NIMH for mood and anxiety, NINDS for
   neurology.
3. Open two or three recent papers from each candidate on PubMed. If the
   abstracts bore you, you have saved yourself a summer.
4. Check they are actually active. A lab whose last paper was 2019 may be
   winding down.
5. Get the email from the faculty page, not from a paper's corresponding-author
   line, which is often stale.

## What a reply looks like

- **"Yes, name me"**: excellent. Put it in the application immediately and say
  so explicitly: *"Dr. X has agreed to host me."* That line is worth a lot. At
  NIH, a yes means the PI will look for your application, so make sure it is
  submitted.
- **"I am not taking students but talk to Dr. Y"**: a warm introduction. Use it.
- **"Apply through the central program"**: neutral, but you are now a name they
  have seen rather than one of 400 files.
- **Silence**: the normal outcome. Not personal. Send more.

## Worth knowing

Expect most emails to go unanswered. Ten emails producing one "yes" is a good
rate, and that one yes can decide the summer. The failure mode is not sending
them at all.
