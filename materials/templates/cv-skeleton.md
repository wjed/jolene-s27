# Research CV: skeleton

One page. Two only if there is genuinely a second page of content, which at this
stage there usually is not.

A research CV is not a job resume. Drop the objective statement, drop "Microsoft
Office", and lead with education and research.

## Rules that matter

- **Reverse chronological** within every section.
- **No pronouns.** "Performed immunostaining," not "I performed immunostaining."
- **Verbs first.** Not "Was responsible for maintaining cell cultures" but
  "Maintained cell cultures."
- **Be specific about skills.** "Proficient in laboratory techniques" says
  nothing. "Mouse colony management, genotyping by PCR" says a lot.
- **Quantify where it is honest.** "Enrolled 40 patients," "analyzed 12 months of
  registry data."
- **One page, 10-11pt, generous margins.** A cramped two-pager reads worse than a
  clean one-pager.
- **Export to PDF**, named `Lastname-Firstname-CV.pdf`. Never send a .docx.

## If the research section is thin

Normal for a rising junior, and not disqualifying, Seattle Children's explicitly
prioritizes first-time research interns, and plenty of others take students with
no bench experience at all.

What to do: promote a serious course project into the research section with its
real question and method. List coursework that signals readiness (statistics,
biochem, a lab-heavy course). Put technical skills higher so they are seen early.
Do not pad with high-school material.
