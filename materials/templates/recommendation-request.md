# Asking for letters of recommendation

Most programs that ask for letters want two, and some want one. Your two are
your PI and the professor of the psychology course you TA. The same two
letters go everywhere, so this is a one-time cost, but only if you ask early
and make it easy.

**Ask as soon as you read this.** The first letters are due in mid-January.
Yale Child Study Center's application, which needs two academic letters,
closes 14 January at 5 p.m. Eastern. Penn's Undergraduate Clinical Scholars
Program wants its letters sent by 15 January at 5 p.m. Eastern, and Seattle
Children's by 19 January. Faculty write letters in batches, and December is
when every premed in the country is asking. A request in November gives them
weeks. One sent in January does not.

---

## Who to ask

In order of usefulness:

1. **Your PI in the Depression and Anxiety Lab.** Nothing else is as
   persuasive, and you have been running participant sessions, coding data
   and troubleshooting protocols in that lab since August 2025. Where a
   program takes one letter, as Seattle Children's does, this is the one.
   If a graduate student or postdoc supervises you day to day, ask whether
   they can write it with your PI, since the detail comes from whoever
   watched you work.
2. **The professor of the intro psychology course you TA.** A 300-student
   lecture is usually a poor source of letters, but you are not one of
   the 300. You work for this professor, who watched you start the review
   sessions on your own.
3. **The instructor of the Biology course you are a Learning Assistant for.**
   They have watched you show up for 80 students and follow through, which is
   most of what a letter is for. They are also your backup: if either of the
   first two says no, or has not answered in a week, ask straight away.
4. **For clinically oriented programs only: the pediatrician you have shadowed
   for more than five years, or an EMS supervisor at Centre LifeLink.** The
   pediatrician can vouch for more than five years of interest in pediatrics,
   a supervisor for your own work on more than 100 emergency calls. Use one as
   a second or extra letter, never in place of your PI's, and only where the
   program does not ask for academic or faculty letters.

Your first two fit the programs that say who should write: Yale Child Study
Center wants two academic letters, Rochester's Strong Children's Research
Center two faculty letters, and Penn's Clinical Scholars Program one from a
professor and one from your research mentor.

Avoid a famous professor who cannot pick you out of a crowd. You have three
people who can. If you ever need a fourth, choose a professor from a small,
hard course where you spoke up, not a big lecture you sat in.

## The email

Ask in person where you can, then send this the same day. This version is for
your PI.

> **Subject:** Letter of recommendation request, summer 2027 research programs
> (first letter due [EARLIEST LETTER DATE])
>
> Dear Dr. [LAST NAME],
>
> I am applying to summer 2027 research programs, mostly in pediatric,
> developmental and clinical research, and I am hoping you would be willing to
> write me a letter of recommendation.
>
> I have been a research assistant in your lab since August 2025. I run
> participant sessions, code data, work on the technology-based emotion
> tracking and troubleshoot study protocols. [ONE THING YOU NOW HANDLE ON YOUR
> OWN, AND SINCE WHEN.]
>
> I am applying to about [NUMBER] programs. The first letter is due
> **[EARLIEST LETTER DATE]** and the last on **[LATEST LETTER DATE]**. Most
> programs collect letters through an online form that emails you a link once
> I submit my part, so you would get one email per program. I have attached the
> list, with each date and how each letter is submitted, and I will send a
> reminder before every deadline so you do not have to track any of it.
>
> I have also attached my CV, my draft personal statement, and a short note on
> the parts of my work you may not have seen.
>
> Would you be willing? If you do not feel you could write a strong letter, I
> would completely understand. I would rather know now.
>
> Thank you for considering it.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

Fill both dates from your own list. If the Broad is on it, the first letter is
due 11 January.

The paragraph asking whether they could write a strong letter matters. It gives
a lukewarm writer a graceful exit, which is far better than a tepid letter you
never see.

For the others, change the greeting to the title each one goes by, and swap
the paragraph about the lab for theirs:

> **The psychology professor:** I have been a teaching assistant for your
> [COURSE NUMBER] since [TERM], and I started its review sessions myself.
> [WHEN THEY RUN AND ROUGHLY HOW MANY OF THE 300 STUDENTS COME.]
>
> **The Biology instructor:** I have been a Learning Assistant for your
> [COURSE NUMBER] since [TERM], working with its 80 students. [ONE THING THEY
> HAVE SEEN YOU DO IN THAT ROLE.]
>
> **The pediatrician:** I have shadowed you since [YEAR]. [ONE THING YOU
> LEARNED WHILE SHADOWING, WITH NOTHING THAT COULD IDENTIFY A PATIENT.]
>
> **An EMS supervisor:** I have volunteered with Centre LifeLink since August
> 2025 and answered more than 100 emergency calls. [ONE CALL OR SHIFT THEY SAW
> YOU HANDLE, WITH NOTHING THAT COULD IDENTIFY A PATIENT.]

A clinical writer is writing for one or two programs, not all of them, so
replace the paragraph about how many programs with their names and dates.

If your [personal statement](../templates/personal-statement.md) is not drafted
yet, send the request anyway with the CV and say the statement will follow
within two weeks. Waiting for a perfect packet costs more than it gains.

## The one list to send each recommender

One page, one row per program, in date order. It is the most useful thing you
can give a recommender, because it turns twenty separate emails into one plan.
Copy this table and fill in your own programs. The date comes from the
program's page on the site, and how the letter is submitted comes from the
program's own page. Where a page gives no separate date for letters, use the
application deadline.

| Program | Letter due | How the letter is submitted |
|---|---|---|
| Yale Child Study Center internship | 14 January, 5 p.m. Eastern (the application deadline) | [a link the portal emails them, an upload, or an email address] |
| Penn Undergraduate Clinical Scholars Program | 15 January, 5 p.m. Eastern, sent or postmarked | [how] |
| Seattle Children's Summer Scholars | 19 January, [time and time zone] | [how] |
| NIH Summer Internship Program | 2 February, noon Eastern | [how] |
| [Program] | [Date, time and time zone] | [how] |

The filled rows are real dates. Keep the ones you are applying to and add the
rest, but only programs you mean to finish: every row is another form for a
writer. If you are not sure a program is worth a row, its fit rating and the
For you line on its page are a quick check.

Three things make the list work:

- **Put the time and time zone on every date.** NIH's letters close at noon
  Eastern on 2 February, and a professor who reads "2 February" will assume
  the end of the day.
- **Give each Amgen host its own row.** Each host runs its own application, so
  five hosts means five requests.
- **Flag the ones where letters are due with the application**, so they get
  done first. Penn SUIP and the University of Maryland's Schnaper program both
  want everything, letters included, by 1 February.

## The packet to attach

Everything that makes writing easier makes the letter better:

1. **CV**: the research version, with the lab first and your three CITI
   certificates on it. The [CV guide](../templates/cv-skeleton.md) has yours
   laid out. Most undergraduates have no CITI training, and a writer who sees
   yours can say so.
2. **Personal statement**: the draft is fine. It tells them the story to
   corroborate.
3. **Their rows of the one list**, not the whole spreadsheet from the site.
   Your PI's has every program. The psychology professor's leaves out the
   programs that take one letter, since that letter is your PI's. A clinical
   writer's has only the programs it is for.
4. **What they have not seen.** Each writer knows one part of what you do. Your
   PI may not know about your 100-plus calls with Centre LifeLink, the summer
   as a medical assistant seeing up to 26 patients a day, or the years of
   shadowing, and your instructors may not know about the lab. One short
   paragraph with those numbers lets each of them write about all of you.
   Professors write a lot of these letters, so this is a kindness, not an
   imposition.
5. **What to emphasize.** For your PI, the sessions you run and the protocols
   you troubleshoot. For the psychology professor, the review sessions you
   started. If a program wants something particular, such as independence or
   clinical research, say so. And tell all of them you have not decided
   between an MD and an MD-PhD: some programs are built for future PhDs, and
   a letter that settles the question for you can work against you there.

## Managing it afterwards

- Add each recommender under **Who is writing your letters** on My
  Applications, and update their status as they reply. Note anything
  program-specific in that program's notes.
- Most portals email the recommender a link only *after* you submit your part,
  so submit early. Seattle Children's closes 4 January and takes letters until
  19 January. Submit it before you leave State College in December, and if its
  portal works this way, your PI has the link before the holidays.
- Send a reminder **ten days before** each deadline if the portal still shows
  the letter as outstanding. For anything due in January, send it before you
  leave State College for winter break instead: professors are hard to reach
  over the holidays.
- Thank them afterwards, and tell them the outcome either way. You will
  probably ask the same people again for medical school.

## The honest timing note

A letter requested on 2 January for a 15 January deadline will be written in a
hurry and will read like it. The same professor, asked in November, writes a
better letter about the same review sessions. The quality difference is
entirely in the lead time.
