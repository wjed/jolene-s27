# Asking for letters of recommendation

Most programs on this list want two letters. The same two letters go everywhere,
so this is a one-time cost, but only if you ask early and make it easy.

**Ask as soon as you read this.** The first letters on this list are due in
January, not February: the Broad's on 11 January, Seattle Children's on
19 January, and NIH's on 2 February. Penn SUIP wants its letters by the
application deadline itself, 1 February. Faculty write letters in batches, and
December is when every pre-med in the country is asking. A request in November
gives them weeks. One sent in January does not.

---

## Who to ask

In order of usefulness:

1. **A research mentor**: anyone who has seen you work in a lab, even briefly.
   Nothing else is as persuasive.
2. **A professor from a small, hard course** where you spoke up and did well.
   A seminar or lab course beats a 400-person lecture every time.
3. **Someone you work for as a learning or teaching assistant.** They have
   watched you show up and follow through, which is most of what a letter is
   for.
4. **A clinical or volunteer supervisor**, if the program is clinically oriented.

Avoid: a famous professor who cannot pick you out of a crowd. A detailed letter
from a postdoc beats a vague one from a department chair.

## The email

> **Subject:** Letter of recommendation request, summer research programs
> (first letter due [EARLIEST LETTER DATE])
>
> Dear Professor [NAME],
>
> I am applying to summer 2027 undergraduate research programs in
> [AREA], and I am hoping you would be willing to write me a letter of
> recommendation.
>
> I took [COURSE] with you in [TERM] and [SPECIFIC THING, the project you did,
> something you contributed, the grade if it is good]. [IF A RESEARCH MENTOR:
> what you worked on and what you handled independently.]
>
> I am applying to about [NUMBER] programs. The first letter is due
> **[EARLIEST LETTER DATE]** and the last on **[LATEST LETTER DATE]**. Most
> programs collect letters through an online form that emails you a link once
> I submit my part, so you would get one email per program. I have attached the
> full list, with each date and how each letter is submitted, and I will send a
> reminder before every deadline so you do not have to track any of it.
>
> I have also attached my CV, my draft personal statement, and a short note on
> what I have been doing since your course.
>
> Would you be willing? If you do not feel you could write a strong letter, I
> would completely understand. I would rather know now.
>
> Thank you for considering it.
>
> [NAME]

Fill both dates from your own list. If the Broad is on it, the first letter is
due 11 January.

That last paragraph of the email matters. It gives a lukewarm writer a graceful
exit, which is far better than a tepid letter you never see.

If your personal statement is not drafted yet, send the request anyway with the
CV and say the statement will follow within two weeks. Waiting for a perfect
packet costs more than it gains.

## The one list to send each recommender

One page, one row per program, in date order. It is the most useful thing you
can give a recommender, because it turns twenty separate emails into one plan.
Copy this table and fill in your own programs. The date comes from the program's
page on the site, and how the letter is submitted comes from the program's own
page.

| Program | Letter due | How the letter is submitted |
|---|---|---|
| Broad Institute BSRP | 11 January, 11:59 p.m. Eastern | [a link the portal emails you, an upload, or an email address] |
| Seattle Children's Summer Scholars | 19 January, 5 p.m. Pacific | [how] |
| NIH Summer Internship Program | 2 February, noon Eastern | [how] |
| Penn SUIP | 1 February, 11:59 p.m. Eastern, same as the application | [how] |
| [Program] | [Date, time and time zone] | [how] |

The four filled rows are real dates. Keep the ones you are applying to and add
the rest.

Three things make the list work:

- **Put a time zone on every date.** Seattle's is Pacific, NIH's is Eastern, and
  a professor reading "5 p.m." will assume their own.
- **Give each Amgen host its own row.** Each host is a separate application and
  sends its own request, so five hosts means five emails.
- **Flag the ones where letters are due with the application**, like Penn SUIP,
  so they get done first.

## The packet to attach

Everything that makes writing easier makes the letter better:

1. **CV**: current.
2. **Personal statement**: the draft is fine. It tells them the story to
   corroborate.
3. **The one list** above: only your programs, with letter dates and how each is
   submitted. Not the whole spreadsheet from the site.
4. **A reminder of who you are**: a short paragraph: the course, the term, what
   you did well, anything memorable. Professors teach hundreds of students a
   year and this is a kindness, not an imposition.
5. **What to emphasize**: if a program wants independence, say so.

## Managing it afterwards

- Add each recommender under **Who is writing your letters** on My
  Applications, and update their status as they reply. Note anything
  program-specific in that program's notes.
- Most portals email the recommender a link only *after* you submit your part.
  Submit early so they have runway. St. Jude explicitly says to submit without
  waiting for letters.
- Send a reminder **ten days before** each deadline if the portal still shows
  the letter as outstanding. For anything due in January, send it before winter
  break instead: professors are hard to reach over the holidays.
- Thank them afterwards, and tell them the outcome either way. They are going to
  be asked again for medical school.

## The honest timing note

A letter requested on 2 January for an 11 January deadline will be written in a
hurry and will read like it. The same professor asked in November writes a
better letter for the same student. The quality difference is entirely in the
lead time.
