# Application materials

Everything in here is a starting point, not a finished document. The parts in
`[SQUARE BRACKETS]` are the parts only you can fill in, and they are the only
parts a reader actually cares about.

## The one idea worth internalizing

Almost every applicant writes some version of *"I have always been passionate
about helping children and I am eager to learn."* Selection committees read
hundreds of those. They are not evidence of anything.

What gets someone selected is **specificity**: a particular question you find
interesting, a particular reason this lab can answer it, and some evidence you
can be useful in a lab for ten weeks. One concrete sentence about a paper you
read beats three paragraphs of enthusiasm.

## What to build, in order

Letters come first, because they are the only thing here that runs on somebody
else's calendar, and the first ones are due in January.

| # | Thing | Time | Reuse |
|---|-------|------|-------|

| 2 | Official transcript | 15 min to order, days to arrive | Every program that insists on an official copy |

## The site around these documents

These guides sit inside a site, and a few parts of it are easy to miss:

- **The Plan** is the page for a normal day: what is open, what is due next,
  what the weekly check found on Monday, and which sites it cannot read.
- **All Programs** has every program, with filters for research area, deadline
  month, housing, stipend and citizenship. Open a program for its eligibility,
  stipend, housing and requirements checklist.
- **My eligibility** takes your year, GPA and citizenship and flags every
  program that conflicts with them, with the reason.
- **My Applications** tracks each application, and its **Who is writing your
  letters** panel keeps track of your recommenders.
- **Back it up.** Your progress is saved in this browser only. Press **Backup**
  at the top of the page once a week and keep the file somewhere safe.
  **Restore** brings it back on a new laptop or after you clear your browser.
- **Add to calendar** downloads every opening date and deadline as a calendar
  file. **Export spreadsheet** downloads the whole list.
- **Your Claude kit** has the files to attach to a Claude chat so it can help
  you fill in forms and draft emails.

## The highest-leverage thing on this whole list

**Cold-emailing PIs.** Most programs let applicants name a preferred mentor, and
many placements are effectively decided by whether a specific faculty member said
"I would take this student." At NIH it is the whole process, because
investigators choose their own interns. A short, specific email to three or four
PIs per program moves the odds more than another round of polishing the personal
statement. [Template here](templates/cold-email-pi.md).
