# Application materials

Everything in here is a starting point, not a finished document, but it starts
from you: the templates are built around your own lab, certificates and
clinical work. The parts in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` are the parts only you can fill
in, such as a story, a name or a number, and they are the parts a reader
actually cares about.

**What the site knows about you.** It is built for you alone, from your lab
work, CITI training, clinical hours, shadowing and teaching, what you want to
study, and the fact that home is in Loudoun County. Every program carries a fit
rating for you (Strong fit, Good fit, Possible or Weak fit), and its
**For you** note says why. Fit is not the same as odds, which each program
shows separately. Programs within reach of home are marked too, since living
at home means no rent.

## The one idea worth internalizing

Almost every applicant writes some version of *"I have always loved working
with children and I am eager to learn."* Selection committees read hundreds of
those. They are not evidence of anything.

What gets someone selected is **specificity**: a particular question you find
interesting, a particular reason this lab can answer it, and some evidence you
can be useful in a lab for ten weeks. You have the raw material for two of
those already. Your question comes from where your lab and your years of
shadowing meet: how mood and anxiety disorders show up in children. Your
evidence is more than a year of running participant sessions, coding data and
troubleshooting study protocols, with CITI training most undergraduates do not
have. The reason a particular lab can answer your question is the part you
research, one program at a time. One concrete sentence about a paper you read
beats three paragraphs of enthusiasm.

## What to build, in order

Letters are the most urgent, because they are the only thing here that runs on
somebody else's calendar, and the first ones are due in January. Each request
goes out with your CV, so the CV comes first, and the skeleton already has your
lab, your certificates and your clinical work in place.

## The site around these documents

These guides sit inside a site, and a few parts of it are easy to miss:

- **The Plan** is the page for a normal day: what is open, what is due next,
  your strongest fits, what the weekly check found on Monday, and which sites
  it cannot read.
- **All Programs** has every program with its fit rating, and filters for
  research area, pay, housing and **Can live at home**. Sort it by fit to see
  your best matches first. Open a program for its For you note, eligibility,
  stipend, housing and requirements checklist.
- **My eligibility** starts with your year and citizenship filled in, and
  nothing on the site rules you out on either. It is also where the site
  explains how it judges fit.
- **My Applications** tracks each application, and its **Who is writing your
  letters** panel keeps track of your recommenders.
- **Back it up.** Your progress is saved in this browser only. Press **Backup**
  at the top of the page once a week and keep the file somewhere safe.
  **Restore** brings it back on a new laptop or after you clear your browser.
- **Add to calendar** downloads every opening date and deadline as a calendar
  file. **Export spreadsheet** downloads the whole list.
- **Your Claude kit** has the files to attach to a Claude chat so it can help
  you fill in forms and draft emails.

## The highest-return thing on this whole list

**Cold-emailing PIs.** Many programs let you name a preferred mentor, and
plenty of placements are effectively decided by whether a specific faculty
member said "I would take this student." At NIH it is the whole process:
investigators choose their own interns, starting in early January, and Bethesda
is a commute from home. Three or four short, specific emails per program you
seriously want, and three to five to NIH labs before winter break, move the
odds more than another round of polishing the personal statement. Start with
the programs rated Strong fit or Good fit. The template already has your
background paragraph, so your fifteen minutes go on the one sentence only you
can write, about their paper. [Template here](templates/cold-email-pi.md).
